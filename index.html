Practice areas

Family law matters rarely arrive one at a time. These issues are usually decided together, in a single case, under the Illinois Marriage and Dissolution of Marriage Act.

Dissolution of marriage

Illinois is a no-fault state: the sole ground for dissolution is irreconcilable differences. The work is rarely about whether the marriage ends — it is about the financial and parenting terms on which it ends.

750 ILCS 5/401

Parental responsibilities

What used to be called custody is now allocated as decision-making authority over education, health, religion, and extracurricular activities — assigned by category, jointly or to one parent, according to the child's best interests.

750 ILCS 5/602.5

Parenting time

A parenting plan sets the regular schedule, holidays, transportation, and communication between households. Relocation of a child's primary residence has its own notice requirements and standards.

750 ILCS 5/602.7, 5/609.2

Child support

Illinois uses an income shares model: support is calculated from both parents' net incomes and the number of overnights, with separate treatment of health insurance, childcare, and extracurricular costs.

750 ILCS 5/505

Maintenance

Whether spousal maintenance is awarded at all comes first; then a statutory formula generally governs amount and duration, with duration tied to the length of the marriage. Higher-income cases can fall outside the guidelines.

750 ILCS 5/504

Property & business interests

Marital property is divided equitably, which does not mean equally. Closely held businesses, professional practices, deferred compensation, and commingled or inherited assets require classification, tracing, and often valuation.

750 ILCS 5/503

Post-decree modification

Support and parenting terms can be revisited when circumstances substantially change — a job loss, a relocation, a new schedule — and enforced when a party simply stops complying with the judgment.

750 ILCS 5/510, 5/610.5

Prenuptial & marital agreements

Agreements made before or during a marriage that set the terms of property and support in advance, drafted to withstand the disclosure and unconscionability challenges that surface years later.

750 ILCS 10/

Orders of protection

Emergency, interim, and plenary orders under the Illinois Domestic Violence Act, and the defense of petitions brought without a factual basis during a contested divorce.

750 ILCS 60/

How a case moves

Every matter is different, but the shape is usually the same. Knowing which stage you are in makes the process considerably less disorienting.

  1. Consultation

    An assessment of the marriage, the finances, the children, and the realistic range of outcomes — before any filing decision is made.

  2. Filing & temporary relief

    A petition opens the case. Where support, exclusive possession, or a parenting schedule cannot wait, temporary orders put terms in place for the duration.

  3. Financial disclosure

    Both sides exchange a sworn financial affidavit and supporting documents. This stage determines what there is to divide, and it decides most cases.

  4. Resolution or trial

    Most matters settle through negotiation or mediation and conclude in a marital settlement agreement and parenting plan. What cannot be resolved is tried.

Common questions

General information about Illinois law, not legal advice about your situation. Statutes and their application change; how any of this bears on your case depends on facts a consultation would cover.

How long does a divorce take in Illinois?

An uncontested case with a full agreement can conclude in a matter of months. A contested case involving a business, disputed parenting, or incomplete disclosure takes considerably longer, and the schedule depends on the assigned calendar and the county. The single largest variable is how quickly both sides produce complete financial documents.

Do I need a reason to file?

No. Illinois abolished fault grounds for dissolution. Irreconcilable differences is the only ground, and a period of separation can establish it conclusively. Marital misconduct is generally not a basis for dividing property or awarding maintenance, though conduct affecting the children or the marital estate can matter for other purposes.

How is property divided?

Property is first classified as marital or non-marital. Marital property is then divided in just proportions after considering statutory factors — the length of the marriage, each spouse's contributions and economic circumstances, obligations from a prior marriage, and others. Equitable division does not mean an even split, and non-marital property that has been commingled may require tracing.

Who decides where the children live?

The parents, if they can agree on a parenting plan; the court, if they cannot. The standard is the best interests of the child, applied through statutory factors that include each parent's participation in past decision-making, the child's needs and adjustment, and the willingness of each parent to support a relationship with the other.

Can support or parenting time be changed later?

Yes, on a showing of a substantial change in circumstances, subject to the statutory limits on when parenting judgments may be modified. Enforcement is a separate track: where a party ignores a support obligation or a parenting schedule, the remedy is a petition to enforce or for contempt, not self-help.

What should I bring to a consultation?

Recent tax returns, pay records, and statements for the significant accounts and debts, along with any existing court papers or agreements. A rough list of assets and their approximate values is enough to start; the documents can follow.
